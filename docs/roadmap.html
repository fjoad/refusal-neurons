Refusal Neurons Roadmap

Kazemi et al. (2026) found one MLP neuron that gates safety refusal in English text. We are testing whether the same neuron gates refusal in other languages and modalities. First we replicate their paper, then we extend it to Arabic.

Where we are
Replication: our search and rerank recover Apple's neuron and pin value on both 8B models; Qwen3-1.7B is a near-tie, as in the paper
Arabic: searching with Arabic prompts gives largely the same top-5 neurons as English, Apple's neuron among them in all three models; one neuron detects Arabic harmful prompts better than Llama-Guard-3 (0.956 vs 0.910)
Next: a free, coherence-aware LLM judge (the paper's K.1 prompt, run locally), then the Arabic attack evaluation

Milestones

The order agreed on 19 Oct. Each step gates the next. Numbers are rough until the judge is in place (refusal is string-matched on 30 prompts).

  1. 0

    Setup on panther

    Done

    refneu env, Qwen3-1.7B / 8B and Llama-3.1-8B cached, Arditi data splits (HarmBench 191 after overlap removal, matching the paper).

  2. 1

    M1 · Check the published neurons

    Done

    Take Apple's neuron IDs, measure harmful vs harmless separation, then pin the neuron and watch refusal drop.

    Qwen3-1.7B L13:F3270 AUROC 0.999 · Qwen3-8B L14:F7924 0.997 · Llama-3.1-8B L11:F4258 1.000. Pinning drives refusal to about 0 on all three.

  3. 1b

    Arabic teaser

    Done

    The English-found Qwen3-8B neuron on 100 parallel MultiJail prompts, English vs human-translated Arabic.

    Fires at the same token with the same strength (−3.10 Arabic vs −3.21 English). AUROC 0.954 vs 0.977. Pinning lowers refusal in both languages.

  4. 2

    M2 · Rediscover the neurons ourselves

    Done

    Gradient × activation search over the first two-thirds of layers. It passed once three settings matched the paper's appendix exactly: the Table 4 refusal phrases, only Table 3's red template tokens, and Arditi's exact 128/128 training sample. The rerank then sweeps the pin value m on HarmBench and keeps the candidate that reaches 90% attack success at the smallest pin.

    Published neuron rank in our top 5: L13:F3270 4th · L14:F7924 5th · L11:F4258 3rd. Activations match Table 5 (for example −4.41 vs −4.41 on Qwen3-8B). Rerank winners: L14:F7924 at +20 and L11:F4258 at −4, exactly the paper's m*. On 1.7B, L10:F244 edges L13:F3270.

  5. 3

    Pick one judge for every language

    Llama-Guard-3

    Llama-Guard-3-8B, free on panther; no API credits for an LLM judge while we explore. Its attack success rate on M1 outputs climbs from 0.03 to 0.97 as the pin grows. Two caveats: it also calls garbled text unsafe, and it doesn't officially cover Arabic.

  6. 4

    M3 · Full evaluation, English first

    Done (Llama-Guard)

    JailbreakBench ASR with the judge, constant and anchor interventions, MMLU and GSM8K cost, XSTest detection AUROC.

    Detection done. XSTest AUROC: Llama neuron 0.963 (paper 0.97), Qwen3-8B 0.901, 1.7B 0.852; Llama-Guard-3 0.975 with recall 0.82 (paper 0.83).

    One pinned neuron jailbreaks JailbreakBench at 0.95 (Qwen3-8B) and 0.93 (Llama), up from 0.04, on par with Arditi's all-layer ablation (0.91–0.95). The constant pin costs 3–7 MMLU points; the anchor brings the cost to about zero (Qwen3-8B MMLU 0.675 → 0.679).

  7. 5

    Search in Arabic

    First results

    Run the search on Arabic prompts. Same neuron, or a different one? This is the core question for Paper A.

    On parallel MultiJail prompts, an Arabic search that targets each model's own Arabic refusal sentence gives largely the same top 5 as the English search: 4 of 5 shared on both Qwen models, 2 of 5 on Llama. Apple's neuron is in both lists for all three models (#1 in Arabic for Llama and Qwen3-1.7B, #3 for Qwen3-8B). It fires at the same token with nearly the same strength: L13:F3270 −5.59 in Arabic vs −6.44 in English, L14:F7924 −3.19 vs −3.24, L11:F4258 +0.56 vs +0.50.

  8. 6

    Modalities

    After Arabic

    Image and speech, using models that share a backbone with our text models, so "the same neuron" means the same layer and index.

Settled decisions

  • Replicate first. Every direction depends on finding the neuron ourselves.
  • Three models, fp16 on V100. fp16 and fp32 gave identical results.
  • Arabic only for now. A full replication plus Arabic is already a paper.
  • Human-translated data. MultiJail and Aya, so translations can't be blamed.
  • One judge for every language. Otherwise English and Arabic numbers aren't comparable.

Open questions

  • Which judge? LLM judge vs translate-then-classify.
  • Which multimodal models share our backbones? Check before choosing.
  • Is MultiJail harmful enough? Baseline refusal is only about 0.45 by string match.

Recommendation

  1. Add a coherence-aware judge: a local LLM with the paper's K.1 prompt, free on panther. It fixes the residual baseline and gives the paper's second judge.
  2. Arabic attacks: pin the neuron on Arabic prompts and judge the responses. That needs a plan for judging Arabic.
  3. Rerun §3.3 with the residual read at each layer's output, as the paper likely did, judged by the coherence-aware judge.
  4. Remaining paper items: §3.5 max-activating text (you review the examples) and §4 concept neurons (optional).
